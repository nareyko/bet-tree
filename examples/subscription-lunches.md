# Teaching example: subscription lunches

This is a classroom business: hot lunches delivered by subscription to offices. Every number below is a **bet written before experience**. None of these numbers is market evidence, a measured result, or a recommendation for a real kitchen.

The funnel in the example starts from 100 showings and a price of 4,500 in the local currency per week. Currency here is the one used in the original teaching deck.

## Basic L-frame (reminder)

The basic level does not fill the whole matrix. For a lived first week, prefer the L-frame in [solo-expert-outreach](solo-expert-outreach.md): write a 3–5 year stake on Business or on Founder (or both), then nest nearer horizons under it. The Week column collects the action sides the week moves.

## One week, written as a contract

**Bet.** If we show a menu at 4,500 to 100 office managers, we get at least 5 requests and at least 3 agreed trials.

**Actions.** Business: 100 showings. Product: menu and bot. Sales: a call within 15 minutes.

**Sample and threshold.** 100 showings. Win line: at least 5 requests from the page and at least 3 trials.

**Guardrail and deadline.** Spend at most 20,000. Form failures = 0. Call within 15 minutes. Look on next Monday morning (seven days).

## Why the signal is early in this example

A paid subscription is weeks away: a trial week, an invoice, a payment. In this classroom story, interest can show in the first 48–72 hours (invented timing, not a method rule). If 100 showings produce 0 requests, the offer missed the pain, and the founder can change it on the next Monday morning after seven days, instead of waiting for the month to end.

## Four outcomes, with this week's lines

| Outcome | Fact in this example | Next decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bet held | At least 5 requests, guardrail intact | Scale: keep the result, or move toward payment |
| Bet failed | Fewer than 5 requests, guardrail intact | Change the offer, the audience, or the price |
| Guardrail broke | Form failures above 0, or spend over the limit | Stop and repair |
| Sample not there yet | For example, 30 of 100 showings done | Finish the showings. Do not change the offer yet |

## A journal row from the teaching story

Week 1 starts with no customer behavior yet. The result cell says there is no data yet. Arguments about architecture, before any customer has acted, are guesses. The decision is to run 100 showings across the seven days and on the next Monday morning read the week-sheet facts and mark one of the four outcomes.

A later teaching row: the bet was that a call within 15 minutes would yield at least 3 trials from 5 requests. The fact was 0 trials from 5 requests, because the calls went out after 4 hours. What was learned in the story: the customer cools within 30 minutes (classroom invention), so speed of contact is the constraint. The decision: connect the bot within 2 hours and call within 5 minutes.

That second row is still a classroom story, not a measured study.
